Cloudflare geoblocking on a Framer site: what actually works

"Use Cloudflare" is the standard answer for blocking countries — and on a Framer site it does work, with caveats that most answers skip. Here's the real setup, where it bites, and when a plugin is the better tool.

Updated July 2026 · 4 min read

The Cloudflare setup, honestly

  1. Move your domain's DNS to Cloudflare. Create a free account, add your site, and switch your nameservers at your registrar. Propagation can take up to a day.
  2. Point the domain at Framer per Framer's custom-domain instructions, and set the records to Proxied (orange cloud). This is the catch — see below.
  3. Create a WAF custom rule: Security → WAF → Custom rules → field Country, operator equals, action Block. The free plan includes a handful of custom rules.

The three catches on Framer specifically

1. WAF only works with the proxy Framer tells you to turn off

Cloudflare's firewall only filters proxied traffic. But Framer's custom-domain guidance recommends DNS-only (grey cloud) mode, because proxying in front of Framer's own SSL and redirects is a known source of certificate errors and redirect loops. You can make proxied mode work (SSL mode "Full", no conflicting rules), but you're now maintaining a config Framer doesn't support if something breaks.

2. Blocked visitors get a Cloudflare error page

A blocked visitor sees Cloudflare's generic 1020/403 screen — Cloudflare branding, error codes, no explanation. Custom block pages are a paid Cloudflare feature. If that visitor is a client, a partner traveling, or a regulator, that's the impression they get of your brand.

3. It's all-or-nothing, domain-wide

The rule blocks the entire domain. You can't block only your pricing page, only a gated section, or show different behavior per page. And every change means logging into a second dashboard that the rest of your Framer workflow never touches.

The alternative: block countries inside Framer

Bouncer is a Framer marketplace plugin that does the same job at the site layer: each visitor is checked server-side on load, and blocked countries see an access screen styled to match your site — your logo, your words. No DNS migration, no proxy, nothing for Framer support to disown. It also does what Cloudflare's free plan doesn't: VPN and proxy detection, per-page rules, and allowlist mode.
