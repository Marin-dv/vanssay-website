Instagram Feed documentation

How to install, configure and use the app. Last updated: September 21, 2026

What it does. Instagram Feed puts a live Instagram feed on your website. You connect a public Instagram handle, pick one of seven layouts, and place the feed on your page. New posts then appear on the published site on their own, with no re-publishing and no manual updates.

It runs as a Webflow App (Designer Extension) and as a Framer plugin. The setup is the same on both; only the insert step differs, and both are covered below.

1. Installing

Webflow

  1. Open your site in the Webflow Designer.
  2. Open the Apps panel in the left toolbar.
  3. Find Instagram Feed and click Install, then Open.
  4. The app opens as a panel on the right side of the Designer.

Framer

  1. Open your project in Framer.
  2. Open the Plugins menu and search for Instagram Feed.
  3. Click Install. The plugin opens in a panel.

No Instagram login is needed. The app reads public profiles by handle. You never connect your Instagram account, and you never enter Instagram credentials anywhere.

2. Connecting a profile

  1. Type or paste an Instagram handle into the field, with or without the @. For example nasa.
  2. Press Preview.
  3. The real profile and its recent posts load so you can see exactly what the feed will look like.

The profile must be public. Private accounts cannot be read by anyone who does not follow them, so they cannot be displayed on a website.

You can preview any handle before creating an account. You only sign in when you are ready to place the feed on your page.

3. Choosing a layout

Seven layouts are available. Click any of them in the panel to see your own posts in that layout straight away.

LayoutWhat it looks like
GridThe classic square gallery, in the number of columns you choose.
ProfileA profile header with avatar, bio and stats, above the posts.
SliderA horizontal row visitors can swipe or drag through.
CarouselA coverflow where side posts scale back for depth.
MarqueeA strip that scrolls continuously on its own.
RainbowA staggered, editorial collage.
ApertureVertical slices that expand as the visitor moves across them.

4. Configuring the feed

All settings live in the app panel and apply immediately to the preview. Nothing here requires code.

SettingWhat it does
ColumnsHow many posts sit side by side.
GapSpace between posts, in pixels.
Corner radiusHow rounded each post is, in pixels.
PostsHow many posts the feed shows. The maximum depends on your plan.
Show headerShows the profile avatar, name and stats above the feed.
CaptionsShows each post's caption under its image.
LightboxOn, a click opens the post in a popup. Off, it opens Instagram in a new tab.

Choosing which posts appear

By default the feed shows your most recent posts and updates itself as you post. On a paid plan you can instead use the curated picker to hand-pick exactly which posts appear, chosen from your latest 50.

5. Inserting and publishing

Webflow

  1. On the canvas, click the Section or the Body where the feed should go.
  2. In the app panel, press Insert.
  3. A labelled placeholder appears on the canvas. This is normal: the Webflow Designer cannot run scripts or load external images, so no feed renders real posts on the canvas.
  4. Press Publish in Webflow. The real feed appears on your published site.

To also see the feed in Webflow Preview, turn on Site Settings → Custom Code → "Run custom code in Preview". This is a one-time setting per site.

Framer

  1. Press Insert in the plugin panel.
  2. The feed is added to your canvas as a code component and renders your posts directly.
  3. Publish your site as usual.

6. How syncing works

Once the feed is on your published site, it keeps itself current. When you post to Instagram, the new post appears on your site automatically. You do not need to re-publish, re-insert or open the app again.

How often the feed checks for new posts depends on your plan. You can also trigger a manual refresh from the app panel, a limited number of times per day.

Posts are fetched and cached on our servers, not in your visitors' browsers, so the feed stays fast and does not slow your page down.

7. Plans and limits

There is a free tier and several paid plans. Plans differ by how many Instagram profiles you can connect, how many posts each feed shows, how many feed views per month are included, how often the feed syncs, and whether the curated picker is available.

The free tier runs on your platform's own address only, meaning *.webflow.io or *.framer.website. To run a feed on a domain you own, you need a paid plan. This is so you can see the product working on a real, public site before paying anything.

Current prices and what each plan includes are on the Instagram Feed product page. A one-time Lifetime option is also available; see the Lifetime conditions of purchase.

8. Managing your account

You create a Vanssay account inside the app panel, using an email and password. The same account works across Vanssay products.

To change your plan, update payment details, download invoices or cancel, go to your account page. Cancelling stops future charges; your feed keeps working until the end of the period you have already paid for.

9. Troubleshooting

The feed shows nothing on my published site

  • Check the profile is public. Private accounts cannot be displayed.
  • If you are on the free tier, check the site is on *.webflow.io or *.framer.website. On a custom domain a free feed does not render.
  • Check whether you have passed your plan's monthly view limit. The app panel shows your usage.

The feed shows a placeholder box in the Webflow Designer

That is expected. The Designer canvas cannot execute JavaScript or load external images, so real posts only appear once you publish, or in Preview with "Run custom code in Preview" enabled.

New Instagram posts are not showing up

Feeds refresh on a schedule set by your plan rather than instantly. You can force an update with the manual refresh in the app panel. If the feed is set to curated rather than automatic, it shows only the posts you picked, and new posts will not be added on their own.

My feed stopped working after I connected a custom domain

The free tier only runs on your platform's own address. Moving to a domain you own requires a paid plan.
