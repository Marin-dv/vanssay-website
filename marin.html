Contact-based 3D scanner

[2024–2025]

TIPE at Fénelon Sainte-Marie (CPGE, Paris) · team of 3 · slides (in French) · code

A home-made 3D scanner modelled on an atomic force microscope. A tip rides over the object and tilts a mirror; a laser bounces off the mirror onto a row of six photodiodes, and which one lights up gives the height at that point. Stepper motors move the object under the tip point by point, on a wooden frame driven by two Arduinos that signal each other.

Heights are calibrated against a reference ramp with k-nearest neighbours, then drawn as a triangle mesh in OpenGL. A scan takes 4 to 5 hours for about 15,000 points, with a measured uncertainty of 180 µm. I wrote most of the code, in C.

RoomSafe

[2021–2022]

European School of Brussels I · team of 2 · 3rd place, International Science Festival of European Schools · code

A web app that estimates the risk of COVID-19 airborne transmission at an indoor event before it happens. The host enters the room size, ventilation, filtration, humidity, guest count, duration and mask use, and gets a score out of 10 plus a graph of how many guests, for how long, stay in the safe zone. Guests can then join the event with their own details and the score updates.

The risk model treats the room air as well mixed: virus-carrying droplets build up from an infectious guest and are removed by ventilation, filtration, settling to the floor and the virus decaying over time. Built with Python, Flask and SQLite.
